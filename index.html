3. The regression behind the simulator

The simulator uses the coefficients reported in the published study.

ln(NIFTY 50) = α − 0.0823 ln(Repo Rate) + 1.5284 ln(Nominal GDP) − 0.2437 ln(CPI) + ε

In this log–log specification, each slope coefficient is an estimated elasticity: it describes the percentage association between a 1% change in an explanatory variable and the NIFTY 50, holding the other included variables constant. These are conditional associations, not causal effects.

Reported adjusted R² = 94.9%. *A displayed p-value of 0.000 is rounded; it should be interpreted as p < 0.001, not literally zero.

Understanding the results

Adjusted R²

A high adjusted R² indicates that the model fits the historical sample well, accounting for the number of explanatory variables. It is not 94.9% predictive accuracy.

p-values

Each p-value assesses evidence against a zero-coefficient null hypothesis, conditional on the specified model. An insignificant result does not prove that the variable has no economic effect.

Association ≠ causation

The regression identifies estimated relationships in the sample. It does not establish that repo rates, GDP or CPI cause movements in the NIFTY 50.

5. Methodology, data and limitations

Research objective. The study investigates the statistical relationship between India's NIFTY 50 and three macroeconomic indicators: the policy repo rate, nominal GDP and the Consumer Price Index. The simulator was built to make the reported regression easier for students and teachers to explore interactively.

Observation period and frequency. The published regression is described as using 24 annual observations spanning March 2001 to March 2024. These are annual observations, not 24 years of monthly observations. The initial ten-year monthly analysis is a separate stage of the wider research and is not the dataset powering this simulator.

Variables and transformation. The model is specified in logarithms (a log–log form). The explanatory variables are repo rate, nominal GDP and CPI; the dependent variable is the NIFTY 50 index. The coefficients can therefore be read as estimated elasticities, holding the other regressors constant.

Data used in the interactive historical comparisons. The six illustrative snapshots in this interface combine March-end NIFTY 50 closes and repo rates, approximate calendar-year nominal GDP in USD billions, and a linked CPI series. The original raw estimation dataset and full estimated intercept are not embedded in the paper information available to this interface. The source and construction of the illustrative snapshot series are therefore not equivalent to a reproducible copy of the paper's estimation dataset.

Why are absolute model levels described as indicative?

The article reports the three slope coefficients but the simulator does not have the original full estimation dataset and estimated intercept. For the interactive display, the intercept is calibrated to the six historical snapshots included here. The absolute model-estimated level is therefore illustrative. The relative change generated when a user changes inputs follows the reported coefficients, but the simulator must not be treated as a faithful replication of the paper's original fitted values or as a forecasting model.

Limitations to keep in mind
  • Small sample: 24 annual observations provide limited degrees of freedom.
  • Potential multicollinearity: macroeconomic variables may move together; this can affect coefficient precision. No diagnostic result is claimed here.
  • Omitted variables: earnings, interest rates beyond the repo rate, exchange rates, global liquidity, investor sentiment and geopolitical shocks can also influence equity prices.
  • Structural change: India's economy and financial markets have changed over the observation period, so relationships may not remain stable.
  • Historical fit is not a future guarantee: a high adjusted R² does not establish out-of-sample forecasting performance.
  • Snapshot comparability: the illustrative inputs use different reference conventions and should not be interpreted as a formal out-of-sample test.
